# Competitor research brief — Ownerdeck

Paste everything below the line into an AI with live web access. It is written
to produce something decidable rather than a wall of observations: every claim
has to be traceable to a page you can open.

---

## The brief

You have web access. Research the competitive landscape for the business
described below and report why the successful players are successful.

### The business you are researching for

**Ownerdeck** builds and then runs the online side of small owner-operated
businesses. Not a one-off build — an ongoing service.

- **What it delivers:** a website, the database behind it, an AI assistant
  answering enquiries on WhatsApp and Instagram DMs and website chat, real
  bookings with deposits and a calendar, a Google Business Profile, and
  follow-up campaigns to past customers. One set of facts drives all of it, so
  a price changes in one place.
- **Pricing:** €600 to build + €99/month (website with an on-site AI chat);
  €1,900 + €249/month (adds the live database, the assistant on WhatsApp and
  Instagram, and bookings); €2,400 + €299/month (adds Google listing and
  follow-up). Optional €75 refundable deposit to hold a build slot. Not VAT
  registered.
- **Who it sells to:** owner-operated businesses that run on bookings — car
  and scooter rental, boat and jetski charter, tours and diving, villas and
  short-term rentals, guesthouses, estate agencies, private clinics, salons.
- **Where:** based in Cyprus, sells mostly to Cyprus, works remotely.
- **Who runs it:** one person. Sole trader. That is the constraint that shapes
  everything — no team to scale delivery with.

### What to find

Cover all five groups. For each, name specific companies with URLs.

1. **Direct competitors** — agencies, freelancers or small studios selling
   "website plus WhatsApp automation" to small businesses. Prioritise Cyprus,
   then Greece, Malta, Spain, Portugal and the wider Mediterranean. Search in
   Greek as well as English; a Cyprus-facing competitor may not rank in
   English at all.
2. **AI chat and WhatsApp automation platforms** — the self-serve tools an
   owner might buy instead (for example Wati, Respond.io, Landbot, ManyChat,
   Chatfuel). These compete on price and lose on setup effort.
3. **Booking software for these exact trades** — for example Checkfront,
   Rezdy, FareHarbor, Peek Pro, Bookeo, Hostaway, Fresha, Treatwell. Note
   which trades each one owns and how it charges.
4. **Website builders aimed at small business** — Squarespace, Wix, GoDaddy
   and any Cyprus web agencies with public pricing.
5. **Productized "done-for-you" services** — anyone selling a fixed-scope
   build plus a monthly retainer, in any market. The model matters more than
   the sector here.

### What to report for each competitor

Be specific. Quote their actual words rather than paraphrasing.

- **The one-line positioning** at the top of their homepage, quoted verbatim.
- **Who they explicitly say they are for**, and who they exclude.
- **Pricing**: the real numbers, the model (one-off, monthly, setup plus
  monthly, per booking, commission), and whether it is public at all. If it is
  hidden behind "contact us", say so — that is itself a finding.
- **What proof they use**: named clients, logos, numbers, case studies,
  testimonials, review scores. Say whether each is verifiable or unfalsifiable.
- **The path from landing to buying**: how many clicks, what they ask for,
  whether there is a free trial, demo, call booking or self-serve checkout.
- **Which objections they answer** on the page — lock-in, data ownership,
  setup time, "will it replace me", cancellation.
- **Where they are weak**: what they do not offer, do badly, or charge too
  much for.

### Then answer the actual question

Do not stop at description. Answer these directly:

1. **What do the successful ones have in common?** Look for the pattern across
   winners, not a list of features. If three of them lead with the same kind of
   claim, that is the finding.
2. **How do they price, and why does that model work for them?** Pay attention
   to who carries the risk — setup fee, free trial, commission per booking.
3. **What proof actually converts in this market?** Named clients with
   numbers, or trust signals of another kind?
4. **What is the strongest argument against Ownerdeck** that a prospect would
   hear from each of these competitors? Write it as they would say it.
5. **Where is the genuine gap?** Something Ownerdeck can own that a
   self-serve platform structurally cannot, and that an agency will not bother
   with.
6. **What should Ownerdeck change?** Concrete: positioning line, pricing
   structure, what the homepage leads with, which objection to answer first.
   Say what to stop doing as well as what to start.

### Rules on evidence

These matter more than coverage. A short honest report beats a long confident
one.

- **Give a URL for every claim.** If you cannot link it, do not assert it.
- **Never invent a price, a client count or a funding figure.** If pricing is
  not public, write "not published" — do not estimate.
- **Separate what you observed from what you inferred.** Mark inferences as
  inferences.
- **Say what you could not check.** Paywalled, geo-blocked, no longer online,
  contradicted by another source — all worth stating.
- **Prefer the company's own live pages** over listicles, "top 10" roundups
  and SEO affiliate content, which are usually stale or paid.
- **Note the date** you checked, since pricing moves.
- If a competitor looks impressive but you cannot verify a single concrete
  claim they make, say that plainly. It is a useful finding.

### Output

1. A comparison table: company, positioning, who it is for, price, model,
   proof used, main weakness.
2. The six answers above, in prose, roughly 150 words each.
3. A short list of the three changes you would make to Ownerdeck first, in
   priority order, each with the evidence behind it.

Keep the whole thing under 2,000 words. If a section is thin because the
evidence is thin, leave it thin and say why.
